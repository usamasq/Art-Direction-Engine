# Art Direction Engine — 01A · Art Direction Creation Guide

*How to invent a distinctive, impactful art direction for any brand or campaign — and turn it into a filled Profile (01). This is the upstream thinking layer: the template tells you what fields exist; this tells you how to fill them with something that isn't generic. Its output is a completed Doc 01.*

---

## 0. What this guide is for

Given any brand or campaign, produce an art direction that is **specific, ownable, and impactful** — never the default AI look (high-saturation, centred hero, plastic gloss, "vibrant modern clean"). The guide works whether a human follows it or an AI runs it.

**Two entry cases:**
- **User specifies a direction** → skip invention; go straight to Phase 4 (Specify) and Phase 5 (pressure-test), refining what they gave you.
- **User does not** → run the full process and *propose*: invent 2–3 distinct candidate territories (Phase 2), recommend one with reasoning, and on selection build the full Profile. Never silently default to one safe look.

---

## 1. The core belief

**Art direction is a position, not a style.** A style is a bag of adjectives anyone can wear. A position is a point of view that exists *in relation to* the category, the competition, and the audience — and that every visual choice can be traced back to. Generic work happens when someone reaches for adjectives ("premium, dynamic, eye-catching") instead of taking a position.

The whole method is: **find the one true organizing idea, then make every sensory decision serve it.** If a choice doesn't serve the idea, it's decoration — cut it.

---

## 2. The process (six phases)

```
1 INTERROGATE  → understand the brand, audience, category, competition, constraints
2 TERRITORY    → find the organizing idea(s); the position; 2–3 candidates
3 CONCEPT      → grow the chosen idea into a visual world + a signature device
4 SPECIFY      → translate into concrete Profile fields (light, palette, texture, world…)
5 DISTINGUISH  → pressure-test against generic; differentiation + impact checks
6 OUTPUT       → the filled Doc 01 Profile + a short rationale
```

### Phase 1 — Interrogate
Gather the substrate. If the user hasn't supplied it, ask only what you can't infer, then proceed.
- **Brand truth:** what is genuinely, specifically true about this brand? Its origin, its place, its material, its ritual, its founder's reason. The look is almost always hiding in here.
- **Audience truth:** who is this for, what do they already feel, what would make *them* (not a generic viewer) stop?
- **The job:** what must the visual make someone feel or do — crave, trust, fear missing out, feel seen, feel awe?
- **Category codes:** list the visual clichés of this category (the things *everyone* in it does). You need them named so you can break them.
- **Competitors:** where do the main rivals sit visually? Map them so you can find open space.
- **Constraints:** non-negotiables — legal, cultural, product truth, channel, what must never appear.

### Phase 2 — Find the territory *(the make-or-break phase)*
A **territory** is the single organizing idea the whole look will serve, stated in one line. Use any of these generators — they reliably produce distinctive territories rather than safe ones:

| Generator | Move | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| **Category-contrast** | name the category's clichés, then invert or sidestep them | a look that stands out *because* it refuses the category default |
| **Competitor white-space** | map where rivals sit; aim for the empty quadrant | differentiation by position |
| **Brand-truth mining** | build the look on the one specific true thing (a place, a material, a ritual) | authenticity that can't be copied |
| **Organizing tension** | name two forces in tension and let the look hold both | depth and a reason for nuance |
| **Unexpected reference** | borrow a visual language from an adjacent, non-obvious world (a film, an editorial era, a craft tradition, an art movement) — disciplined, not pastiche | a fresh register the category hasn't worn out |
| **Constraint dare** | impose one hard rule (one light source; no faces; only natural materials; a 3-colour palette) | distinctiveness forced by limitation |

Write each candidate territory as **one sentence with a reason-to-be**, e.g.:
> *"Quiet-luxury heritage — soft natural light and matte earth tones, because the brand's truth is generational warmth, and the loud competitor has left calm confidence wide open."*

Produce **2–3 candidates** that are genuinely different from each other (not three flavours of the same idea). Recommend one and say why.

### Phase 3 — Concept the look
Grow the chosen territory into a visual world. Decide:
- **Emotional register:** the dominant feeling (crave / trust / awe / urgency / belonging).
- **The world:** where this lives — real places, light, time of day, materials.
- **The signature device** *(critical for ownability):* one repeatable visual move that makes the work recognisable at a glance — a recurring framing, a lighting motif, a colour behaviour, a compositional rule, a texture. A feed becomes a brand when a device repeats. Name it explicitly.
- **The contrast engine:** what creates attention — tonal, scale, colour, or focus contrast. Impact comes from contrast, not from cramming.

### Phase 4 — Specify (→ the Profile fields)
Translate the concept into the concrete Doc 01 fields. Each choice must be sensory and specific — replace every adjective with a decision.

| Profile field | How to derive it from the concept |
|---|---|
| F2 Reject → Target | the category clichés (Phase 1) become the Reject; the territory becomes the Target |
| F3 Palette | a *tight* set with roles and a saturation stance; assign each colour a meaning |
| F4 Lighting | one signature light (source, direction, time, hardness) + a forbidden lighting |
| F5 Texture | the real materials of the world; a finish bias; forbidden surfaces |
| F6 World | cast, settings, props, cultural precision — specific, rotating lists |
| F7 Modes | if the job needs more than one register (e.g. trust vs crave), declare each mode |
| F8 Composition | framing bias, depth of field, the signature device as a rule, forbidden moves |
| F9 Negative space | the in-palette defocused treatment (never white) — tied to the world |
| F10 Hard constraints | the Phase 1 non-negotiables |
| F11 Drift-watch | the specific ways *this* direction will get watered down toward generic |

> **Rule of specificity:** if a line could be pasted into a different brand's profile unchanged, it's not direction yet. Make it concrete enough that it could only be this brand.

### Phase 5 — Distinguish & pressure-test
Run the direction against these before shipping it:
- **The swap test:** would this direction work, unchanged, for a competitor? If yes → it's generic. Sharpen until it's brand-specific.
- **The adjective audit:** strike every "vibrant / modern / clean / dynamic / premium / eye-catching" and replace with a concrete sensory decision. Adjectives are placeholders for direction not yet done.
- **The default-AI check:** is it leaning on high-saturation, centred hero, plastic gloss, studio glow, stock smiles? If so it's the machine's default, not yours.
- **The one-idea check:** can every major choice be traced to the territory? Cut what can't.
- **The tightness check:** roughly ≤5 core moves. A direction is a tight system, not a mood board of everything.
- **The impact check:** is there a single clear focal idea, a contrast engine, and an emotional register matched to the job?
- **The medium check:** does it survive in motion, in a grid, and at thumbnail size?

### Phase 6 — Output
Produce the **filled Doc 01 Profile** (every field, no blanks except style-lock handles you can't know yet), preceded by a **3–5 line rationale**: the territory, why it beats the generic option, the signature device, and what it's positioned against. Then the rest of the system (02–06) runs on it.

## 3. The anti-generic doctrine (quick reference)

What generic looks like → the antidote:
- **Adjective soup** ("vibrant, modern, premium") → concrete sensory decisions.
- **No point of view** → a position defined against the category/competition.
- **Everything included** → a tight system of ≤5 moves and a forbidden list.
- **Stock signifiers** (generic "diverse smiling people", "Middle Eastern", "luxury") → specific cultural and material truth.
- **The current AI default** (sat-pop, centred, glossy) → a signature device + a single light rule + restraint.
- **Could-be-anyone** → passes the swap test only by being unmistakably this brand.

Impact is bought with **contrast, restraint, and a single clear idea** — not with more saturation or more elements.

---

## 4. How an AI should run this guide

```
- If the user named a direction: refine and SPECIFY it (Phase 4–5). Don't override their call.
- If they didn't: run Phases 1–2 and PROPOSE 2–3 distinct territories, each one line
  with a reason-to-be, and recommend one. Wait for the pick, then build the full Profile.
- Always do the swap test and the adjective audit before outputting.
- Output = a short rationale + the filled Doc 01 Profile. Clean by default (Operating
  Contract, Doc 00): no narration of the phases unless the user asks to see the thinking.
- Ask only for brand truths you genuinely can't infer; make reasonable assumptions and
  state them in one line rather than interrogating the user.
```

> **This "propose, don't assume" reflex isn't only for inventing a brand profile.** It applies just as much when a *scene* brief is thin (e.g. "make me a cool reel for the juice, something fun"): ask 2–3 questions or propose 2–3 distinct scene directions before locking a full Scene Sheet — see Operating Contract §4.3a. Invent freely *within* a chosen direction; never choose the direction silently.

## 5. Worked example (condensed) — a new hydration-drink brand

*Shows the reasoning producing something non-generic. Fictional brand.*

- **Phase 1 — Interrogate.** Category codes: neon brights, sweaty gym hero shots, electrolyte-blue splashes, aggressive sans-serif, "performance" everything. Competitors all sit in the same loud-sporty quadrant. Brand truth: it's made for *recovery and calm hydration*, not gym maxing. Audience: burnt-out adults who want to feel restored, not pumped. Job: make hydration feel like *relief and care*.
- **Phase 2 — Territory (candidates).**
  1. *"Clinical calm"* — pharmacy-grade restraint, cool whites, because the category is chaos and trust is open. 
  2. **"Morning-light recovery"** *(recommended)* — soft dawn light, real water, skin and breath, because the brand's truth is restoration and the whole category has abandoned calm for neon. 
  3. *"Elemental"* — water/salt/mineral as raw still-life, because it dramatises the actual ingredients.
- **Phase 3 — Concept.** Register: relief. World: early-morning interiors and bathrooms, condensation on a cold glass, bare skin, soft towels, a window. **Signature device:** every shot has a single soft dawn light source from one side and a wet, cool surface catching it. Contrast engine: cool wet highlight against warm soft skin and matte interior.
- **Phase 4 — Specify (extract).** Reject: neon, gym sweat, blue splash, hard studio light. Target: dawn-lit recovery realism. Palette: warm linen `#EDE3D6`, cool water-grey `#AEB9BD`, skin neutrals, one restrained mineral-blue accent; low saturation. Light: single soft dawn window light, soft shadows; forbidden: hard multi-source studio. Texture: condensation, damp skin, matte ceramic, soft cotton; matte bias. Negative space: the defocused dawn-lit wall, never white.
- **Phase 5 — Pressure-test.** Swap test: would this work for a neon competitor? No — it's built on the recovery truth. Adjective audit: "calm" → "single soft dawn light, low saturation, matte surfaces." Default-AI check: deliberately rejects sat-pop and centred hero.
- **Phase 6 — Output.** Rationale + filled Profile, positioned squarely against the loud-sporty category.

Note how the output could *only* be this brand — that's the test passing.
