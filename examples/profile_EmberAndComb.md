# Art Direction Profile — Ember & Comb (fictional teaching brand)

*A worked instance of [Doc 01](../docs/01_Art_Direction_Profile_TEMPLATE.md), produced by running the [Creation Guide (01A)](../docs/01A_Art_Direction_Creation_Guide.md) on a made-up brand. Use it to see what a filled Profile looks like and what "specific, not generic" means in practice. Pair it with the [worked reel](worked_reel_EmberAndComb.md), which is built from this Profile. Everything here is invented — no real brand.*

---

## Rationale *(the Creation-Guide output that precedes the Profile — Doc 01A Phase 6)*

**Territory:** *Heat × comfort.* Ember & Comb is chili honey, and the one true tension in the product is that it is **both** soothing (honey, warmth, the hearth) **and** sharp (chili, the bite). The whole look holds those two forces at once: glossy amber warmth lit like a fireside, with the ember-red of the chili as the thing that interrupts the comfort. **Why it beats the generic option:** the condiment category defaults to bright, clinical, overhead "foodie" packshots and cartoon-chili mascots — loud and flat. By going *dark, warm, and hearth-lit*, Ember & Comb owns a quadrant the category has abandoned, and the look can only be read as premium and made-by-hand. **Signature device:** every frame has a single warm low light from one side and a thick glossy amber highlight running down the jar or the drizzle — a fireside glint that repeats until the feed is recognizable at a glance. **Positioned against:** supermarket-condiment gloss and novelty hot-sauce kitsch.

## FIELD 1 — Brand snapshot
- **Brand / campaign:** Ember & Comb — small-batch chili honey.
- **One-line positioning:** "Honey with a bite" — craft chili honey for people who drizzle it on everything.
- **Audience:** home cooks and food-lovers 25–45 who buy craft pantry goods; the people who already keep three hot sauces and a good olive oil on the counter.
- **What the visuals must make a viewer feel / do:** crave it on something warm *right now*; read it as handmade and premium, not a novelty gift-shop product; feel the cosy-plus-kick contradiction in one look.

## FIELD 2 — The anti-aesthetic (Reject → Target)
- **Reject:** bright supermarket-condiment gloss; clinical white-background studio packshots; flat overhead "foodie flatlay" light; cartoon-chili mascots and flame graphics; sticky pastel cuteness; high-saturation orange everything; plastic CGI sheen.
- **Target:** hearth-lit craft still-life — one warm low light source, glossy amber against char-dark matte surfaces, real food, real hands, shallow depth of field, a sense of a dark kitchen at dusk.

## FIELD 3 — Palette
| Role | Name | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas / base | Charcoal | `#2A2422` |
| Accent 1 | Amber-honey | `#C8841E` |
| Accent 2 | Ember-red | `#9E3B22` |
| Neutral / negative-space fill | Warm Ash | `#D8CFC2` |
| Secondary neutral | Worn Oak | `#6B4F32` |
| Special / prestige | Beeswax-gold | `#E6B964` |
- **Saturation stance:** rich but warm — never neon, never the category's hot-orange pop. Deep and glowing, not bright.
- **What each accent means:** amber-honey = the product itself (the glossy pour); ember-red = the chili heat, used as a deliberate interruption (a fleck, a glow), never as a wash; beeswax-gold = the brand's prestige/label glint; charcoal is the canvas the warmth sits against, not the mood. Fill negative space with Warm Ash, never pure white.

## FIELD 4 — Lighting signature
- **Primary light:** a single low warm source from one side — hearth/firelight, or a low window at dusk; soft-edged shadows, a gentle amber glow, a little smoke/haze in the air.
- **Mood of shadows:** deep, warm, soft-edged; chiaroscuro that lets the amber highlight pop against the dark.
- **Forbidden lighting:** flat multi-source studio floods; bright overhead daylight; cold or blue light; the "even foodie-blog" look.

## FIELD 5 — Texture & materials
- **Mandated textures:** thick glossy viscous honey mid-drip; chili flecks suspended in the amber; raw honeycomb; worn oak boards; seasoned cast iron; coarse linen; a dusting of dried chili.
- **Surface finish bias:** glossy on the honey *only*; matte on everything else (wood, iron, linen, plaster).
- **Forbidden surface looks:** plastic sheen, CGI gloss on surfaces, waxy skin on hands, over-polished studio reflections.

## FIELD 6 — Subject / world vocabulary
- **People / cast (rotate — the brand is about sharing warm food):** a cook's hands drizzling; a friend reaching across a table; two people mid-meal; a small gathering passing a board. Candid, warm, unposed. Real hands, a little mess.
- **Settings / environments:** a dark rustic kitchen at dusk; a hearth / wood-fired oven; a worn communal dining table; a pantry shelf in low light.
- **Signature props / hero objects:** the Ember & Comb jar + a wooden honey dipper; warm food that wants honey (fried chicken, a baked cheese, warm biscuits/cornbread, charred pizza, roasted carrots); cast iron, a linen napkin, a scattering of dried chilies.
- **Cultural / regional precision rules:** keep it specific and lived-in, not a generic "rustic foodie" set — real worn objects with use-marks, a real plated dish, never a sterile staged tablescape or a flame-graphic gimmick.

## FIELD 7 — Mode dial
| Mode name | Use it for | Light | Color energy | Texture/feel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hearth** | brand story, founder/process, gifting, lifestyle, "the table" posts | warm low firelight, deep shadow, smoky glow | muted charcoal + warm amber, low saturation | matte wood/iron/linen, intimate, still |
| **Drizzle** | product hero, the pour, recipe/craving posts | tighter warm side light catching the glossy honey | amber + a deliberate ember-red cue, richer | glossy viscous honey, suspended flecks, appetite-forward macro |
- **Rule for which mode applies:** story / people / brand-feeling content → Hearth; any post whose job is to make someone crave the product on food → Drizzle. Both keep the single warm light, the dark surfaces, and real texture; they differ only in how tight and glossy the framing is.

## FIELD 8 — Composition defaults
- **Framing bias:** asymmetric, subject off-centre, low and close; a sense of leaning in over the food.
- **Depth of field:** shallow — the honey/jar sharp, the dark kitchen falling out of focus behind.
- **Forbidden:** dead-centre overhead flatlay; static symmetry; cluttered tablescapes with no breathing room.

## FIELD 9 — Negative-space treatment
- **Negative space is:** a defocused, in-palette region of the actual scene — **never a flat white box**.
- **Default fill for this brand:** a soft-shadowed charcoal plaster wall, or the out-of-focus warm-dark kitchen behind the subject, in Warm Ash / Charcoal.
- **Where it usually sits:** upper area or one side in 4:5; lower-third in 9:16. Low-contrast so warm-toned type sits cleanly. Reposition per aspect.

## FIELD 10 — Hard constraints (never-do)
- No cartoon chili / flame mascots or novelty-gift kitsch.
- No bright white studio backgrounds or flat overhead daylight.
- No neon or hot-orange oversaturation.
- No engine-rendered label or legal text — type and the logo are composited in post.
- No plastic CGI sheen on surfaces; honey may be glossy, surfaces stay matte.

## FIELD 11 — Drift-watch (this brand's common failure modes)
- Honey rendering thin and runny instead of thick, slow, and glossy — the pour must look *heavy*.
- The warmth tipping into cartoonish orange oversaturation instead of deep amber glow.
- Losing the chili "heat" cue entirely so it reads as plain honey — keep a deliberate ember-red fleck/glow.
- Reverting to bright, flat, overhead foodie-flatlay light (the category default).
- Negative space rendering as a flat white panel instead of in-palette warm-dark scene.
